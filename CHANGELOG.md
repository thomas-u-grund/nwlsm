# Changelog

## [1.0.6] - 2026-11-22
- Fixed: heap overflow in the optimiser (`mkl_lbfgs`) for networks with fewer than 10 position coordinates (3 to 6 actors): could crash Stata (0xC0000374 on Windows) and may corrupt memory elsewhere. The work space of the L-BFGS memory (10 values) was carved from a block of 5 x P doubles, P being the number of coordinates, and overflowed for P < 10. Results for networks that did not crash are unchanged. The plugins of all platforms are rebuilt from the fixed source. New test `cscripts/test_nwlsm_small.do` (3 to 6 actors, directed and undirected, several seeds).
- Changed: the default number of threads (`threads(0)`) is now 4 threads for one chain and 2 threads per chain for several chains, at most one per processor core (it was one per core, all of them working inside the chain). The estimates are identical for every number of threads. A single chain with 200 or more actors is faster on computers with many logical cores (measured on Windows: 47.6 to 31.3 s at 200 actors, 154 to 125-133 s at 400 actors); no change was measured on macOS and Linux. The error text of `threads()` states the default. New test `cscripts/test_nwlsm_threads_default.do`.
- Added: a network with no ties, or with every possible tie (binary; for `family(poisson)` and `family(binomial)` all observed values 0; diagonal ignored, missing dyads left out), prints a warning that the data carry no information on the intercept and its estimate is determined by the prior. New stored result `e(degenerate)` (1 or 0). Estimates and error codes unchanged. Test in `cscripts/test_nwlsm_small.do`.

## [1.0.4] - 2026-11-21
- `estat mcmcdiag`: the Geweke z is now computed as R does, as `geweke.diag(x, frac1 = 0.1, frac2 = 0.5)` of the package coda: z = (mean1 - mean2) / sqrt(s1/n1 + s2/n2) for the first 10 percent and the last 50 percent of the draws of a chain, with the spectral density at frequency zero of each part estimated as `spectrum0.ar()` does (autoregression by the Yule-Walker equations, order by AIC up to 10 log10(n), s = innovation variance / (1 - sum of the coefficients)^2). The printed z values change (1.0.3 used the autocorrelation time of the whole chain for both parts). Agreement with coda 0.19-4.1: 1e-10 or better on the chains of a four-chain fit and on simulated series.
- The Geweke z now equals `coda::geweke.diag()`. The flag and the verdict line of 1.0.3 are removed (`r(geweke_flag)` and `r(geweke_crit)` no longer exist; the Bonferroni limit is gone too) because the 1.96 limit is not a 5 percent test with several chains: in settled runs about 16 to 26 percent of the z values exceed it (48 settled fits with 4 and 8 chains; with one chain of 4000 draws 5 of 48), since the first 10 percent of a short chain are a few dozen draws and the autoregressive spectral density of such a part is noisy. The Geweke z is informational only: the footnote counts the z values above 1.96 of the k (parameters times chains), says that about 0.05 k are expected with long settled chains and more with short ones, and tells the user to read z with the trace plots and the effective sample sizes. New `r(geweke_nexceed)` and `r(geweke_z)` (chains by parameters); `r(geweke_max)` and `r(geweke_ntests)` as before. The table shows the z of largest size over the chains per row; with 2 to 8 chains a second table shows the z of every chain, as R prints it. A z is missing (coda: NaN) for a series that does not move and is left out of the counts. The warning for an effective sample size below 100 stays.
- Mata library rebuilt (Stata 17): `lsm_geweke()`, new `lsm_spec0()` and `LsmGewekeZ()`. Test `cscripts/test_nwlsm_mcmcdiag.do`: values of coda for fixed series, the counts and `r(geweke_z)`, a binomial band for one chain, no wrapped output at linesize 80.

Tested on macOS with Stata 17 BE: `test_nwlsm_mcmcdiag` (new), `test_nwlsm_plots`, `test_nwlsm_errors`, `test_dlg_plots`, `test_nwlsm_edgecov` and the dialog assembly test (12 of 12 cases). On Linux (Ubuntu 22.04) and Windows 11 (both StataNow 19.5 MP): the same tests (the dialog assembly test needs a GUI Stata and was run on the Mac only) and a by-hand check of `estat mcmcdiag`. Stata 18 not run.

## [1.0.3] - 2026-11-20
- `estat mcmcdiag`: the Geweke warning no longer raises false alarms with several chains. The printed z of a row is still the one of largest size over the chains; the warning compares the largest |z| of all rows with the two-sided 5 percent normal quantile with Bonferroni correction for the number of z values (parameters times chains, for example 2.87 for 3 parameters and 4 chains) instead of 2, and a warning is shown when the smallest effective sample size is below 100. The variance of the two segment means of the z uses the autocorrelation time of the whole chain (the effective sample size of a short first segment was estimated too high, so z was too large in settled multi-chain runs). New stored results `r(geweke_max)`, `r(geweke_crit)`, `r(geweke_ntests)`, `r(geweke_flag)`, `r(ess_min)`, `r(ess_flag)`.

## [0.7.0] - 2026-11-19
- `estat bic`: the BIC of latentnet's `bic.ergmm()` for binary networks (Euclidean distance), computed at the minimum Kullback-Leibler positions and effects: ties given the positions (coefficients maximising the likelihood for those positions, penalty `p log(effective observations)`; `effobs(ties | dyads | actors)`), positions (normal likelihood without clusters; with `clusters()` the EM fit of a mixture of spherical normals with free variances and latentnet's penalty) and the random effects. `r(bic)` and its parts.
- `nwlsmselect`: fits the model for several numbers of clusters (`clusters(0 2 3 4)`, 0 = none), computes the BIC of each and lists the fits ranked by BIC; `refit` makes the best model the active one; results `r(table)`, `r(best)`. Help file `nwlsmselect.sthlp`.
- Agreement with latentnet 2.12.0: the BIC components computed from latentnet's own minimum Kullback-Leibler positions and effects are identical to latentnet's (ties part, effects parts, no-cluster positions part to 1e-3; Sampson monks, directed, and a planted-cluster network, undirected). In complete fits the overall BIC differs from latentnet's by at most 0.5 per cent (Sampson 382.2 against 382.7 without clusters, 373.8 against 372.6 with two and 337.7 against 336.8 with three clusters; planted-cluster network 833.8 against 833.6, 776.6 against 777.3, 654.1 against 656.9) and the ranking over the number of clusters is the same. Deviations (documented): our EM fit of the mixture can end in a better local maximum than latentnet's (planted-cluster network with four clusters: positions part 219.6 against 228.5 on latentnet's own positions); with sender and receiver effects the minimum Kullback-Leibler fit of the monk network has two local optima (sender and receiver effects of about 0.27 and 0.68 standard deviation, BIC about 398 to 402; or about 0.65 and 0.75, BIC about 440): latentnet 2.12.0 ends in the second in 3 of 12 seeds (BIC 455, 462 and 602 in the larger cases, 483 for seed 1) and nwlsm with one start in 3 of 8 seeds (442.8 for the seed of the reference); the first optimum has the higher objective, and 4 starts find it in all 16 seeds tried (2 starts: 14 of 16; 3: 15 of 16). Deliberate deviation from latentnet: `mklstarts()` now defaults to 4 when the model has `sender`, `receiver` or `sociality` (1 otherwise, results without effects unchanged); BIC with effects 396.9 to 399.5 against latentnet's 399.2; cost about 0.2 per cent on the monks (1.18 s) and on a directed network of 100 actors (29.4 s against 29.6 s) because the starts run in parallel. The BIC code itself is exact (see above), so the difference is the local optimum of the fit, not the BIC.
- `estat gof` for valued networks: `cutoff(#)` makes the structure statistics (degree, in- and out-degree, shared partners, geodesic distances) those of the network of the dyads whose value exceeds `#` (default 0 for the Poisson and binomial families, as before); after `family(normal)` they are available when `cutoff()` is given. Observed and simulated networks are cut alike.
- Persistent worker team for the passes over all dyads (updates of the coefficients, minimum Kullback-Leibler objective): the helper threads are created once per chain (or per fit) and sleep between passes instead of being created for every pass, so threads pay from networks of about 40 actors on (before: about 100). Results are unchanged for a fixed seed and identical for every `threads()`. n = 100 undirected network, one chain, 4 threads: 2.1 s against 2.5 s for 1000 draws.
- The plugin protocol is 6; the plugins for Linux and Windows must be rebuilt by CI (the macOS plugin is rebuilt).
- Dialogs: `bic`, `cutoff()` and the effective number of observations in `nwlsm_estat.dlg`.
- Help: worked example with the Lazega lawyers (`nwwebuse lazega`) in `nwlsm.sthlp`, `nwlsmselect.sthlp`.
- Tests: `test_nwlsm_bic` (against latentnet, `dev/ref_latentnet_bic.R`), `test_nwlsm_gof` (cutoff against an independent computation), `test_nwlsm_threads` (team: identical for 1, 2, 4, 8 threads and equal to the numbers of 0.6.0).
- Not implemented: the hierarchical influence model for several networks and the susceptibility indicator of Sweet and Adhikari 2020 (section 5, section 7), see TODO.md.

## [0.6.0] - 2026-11-19
- Latent space model for social influence (Sweet and Adhikari 2020, Psychometrika 85: 251-274), as an option of `nwlsm`: `influence(outcome lag [covariates])`, `neighbors(#)` (the G nearest actors in the latent space; default all), `weight(invdist | expdist)` (weights proportional to 1/distance as in the paper, or exp(-distance)), `obvar()` and `osigma()` (priors of the outcome coefficients and the error variance). The tie model and a Gaussian outcome model (outcome on a constant, the lagged outcome, other regressors and the distance-weighted mean of the others' lagged outcomes) are sampled jointly: Metropolis-Hastings steps for the positions with the change of the outcome log likelihood in the acceptance ratio (O(n) per move), Gibbs steps for the outcome coefficients and the error variance, all in the native plugin, with chains and threads (results identical for every `threads()`). It works with the covariates, random effects, clusters and families of the tie model; it cannot be combined with `bilinear`.
- Results: `e(b)`, `e(V)` (equations `network` and `outcome`), `e(summary)`, `e(rhat)`, `estat mcmcdiag` include the outcome coefficients; `sigma2_y` in `e(vsummary)`; `e(W)` (posterior mean weight matrix), `e(pred)`, `e(prob_positive)`, `savedraws()` with the outcome draws. `estat influence` (influence effect, probability of a positive effect, R-squared with and without the influence term, actors exerting most influence, `predict()`, `exposure()`, `exerted()`, `graph`); `nwlsmplot, size(influence)`.
- Deviations from the paper (documented in the help): the intercept of the tie model is estimated, not fixed at 2; the position variance has latentnet's prior; the outcome coefficients and the error variance are Gibbs steps instead of random-walk steps; default prior of the error variance inverse gamma(0.01, 0.01); tie covariates allowed; no hierarchical model for several networks and no susceptibility indicator.
- Dialogs: influence group in `nwlsm.dlg`, `influence` in `nwlsm_estat.dlg`, `size(influence)` in `nwlsmplot.dlg`.
- Tests: `test_nwlsm_infl_check` (outcome likelihood, weights and incremental updates against direct computation), `test_nwlsm_infl_rec_n50`, `_n100`, `_all` (simulation from the exact model, coverage of 95% intervals), `test_nwlsm_infl_nam` (against the exposure model with a fixed row-normalised weight matrix from `nwspmatrix` and `spregress`), `test_nwlsm_infl_post`.
- Validation (simulation from the exact model of the paper, 30 replications per scenario, 95% posterior intervals; ties by latent distance, outcome by the weighted lagged outcomes, true influence effect 1): n = 50, 10 nearest actors, inverse-distance weights: coverage of the influence effect 1.00, mean posterior mean 1.023 (sd over replications 0.35), coverage of the other outcome coefficients 0.93 to 1.00, error variance 0.97, correlation of true and estimated distances 0.92; n = 100: influence coverage 1.00, mean 1.016 (sd 0.17), others 0.93 to 0.97, distance correlation 0.97; n = 50, all other actors, exp(-distance) weights, undirected: influence coverage 0.90, mean 0.956 (sd 0.48: weak identification with all actors as neighbours). Against least squares with the fixed row-normalised observed network (`nwspmatrix`, `spregress`), 20 replications, n = 100: when the true weights are the observed network (b = 0.7) the fixed-network model gives 0.683 and the influence model 0.478 (same sign, attenuated because the latent-distance weights only approximate the observed network); when the weights depend on the latent positions (b = 1) the influence model gives 1.006 (rmse 0.11) and the fixed-network model 0.457 (rmse 0.57).
- The plugins for Linux and Windows must be rebuilt by CI (the macOS plugin is rebuilt).

## [0.5.0] - 2026-11-19
- Valued networks: `family(poisson | binomial | normal)` with `trials()` (number, network or matrix) for the binomial family and `dispvar()`, `dispdf()` (prior of the dispersion, defaults 1 and 3) for the normal family, for the Euclidean and bilinear terms with covariates, sender, receiver, sociality effects and clusters. Likelihoods, Metropolis updates, a Gibbs step for the dispersion and the minimum Kullback-Leibler fit (family-specific divergence of the posterior mean expected values; scaled by the posterior mean dispersion for the normal family) are in the native plugin. `e(P)` holds expected values; results `e(family)`, `e(trials)`, `e(dispvar)`, `e(dispdf)`; the dispersion is a row of `e(vsummary)`, `e(rhat)` and `savedraws()`. A valued network without `family()` is refused unless all ties equal 1.

Deviation: latentnet 2.12.0 simulates undirected networks with too many ties, nwlsm draws one value per pair.
- `estat ppcheck`: calibration of the posterior mean predictions in groups, Brier score, AUC, log loss (binary) or RMSE, correlation, MAE (valued), `graph`.
- `estat mcmcdiag, graph`: trace plots by chain with the Gelman-Rubin statistic.
- Dialogs: family, trials and dispersion prior in `nwlsm.dlg`; gof, ppcheck and graph in `nwlsm_estat.dlg`.
- Tests: `test_nwlsm_valued` (latentnet agreement, Poisson, binomial, normal, directed and undirected), `test_nwlsm_check_valued`, `test_nwlsm_recovery_valued`, `test_nwlsm_valued_misc`, `test_nwlsm_gof` (against latentnet's gof statistics and distributions).

## [0.4.0] - 2026-11-19
- `mklstarts()` now defaults to 1: the minimum Kullback-Leibler fit starts from the single closest draw, as in latentnet; several starts remain an option. The very weak ridge of the MKL fit is kept.
- Threads and chains, built into the plugin. `chains(#)` runs independent chains in parallel and pools their draws (`nsample()` is the total over all chains; each chain has its own burn-in and tuning; chain k has the seed `seed()` + 1000003 k; chains after the first start from over-dispersed positions). `estat mcmcdiag` shows the Gelman-Rubin statistic (also `e(rhat)`), effective sample sizes summed over the chains and the largest Geweke z. `threads(#)` (default: one per core) distributes the chains over threads; spare threads work inside a chain on the passes over all dyads (coefficient updates) and on the minimum Kullback-Leibler fit in networks of about 100 actors or more. Results for a given seed and `chains()` are identical for every `threads()` (`test_nwlsm_threads`). pthreads on macOS and Linux, `_beginthreadex` on Windows.
- Latent clusters: `clusters(G)` (G = 2 to 6), latentnet's mixture of spherical normals for the positions with its priors; memberships, cluster means, variances and mixing proportions are sampled by Gibbs steps; labels are corrected over the draws. Results `e(Q)`, `e(class)`, `e(cmeans)`, `e(lambda)`, one variance per cluster; `estat clusters`, `nwlsmplot, clusters`.
- Dialogs: `nwlsm.dlg`, `nwlsmplot.dlg`, `nwlsm_estat.dlg`.
- `savedraws()` also saves the variance of each cluster and the mixing proportions; `LsmSample()` returns `"class"`, `"lambda"` and `"chains"`.
- Tests: `test_nwlsm_clusters` (planted clusters, ARI against truth and latentnet, coefficients against latentnet), `test_nwlsm_threads`; the earlier tests were rerun with the new defaults. Run so far: Stata 17 BE on macOS only.
- Not implemented: valued networks (Poisson, binomial, Gaussian).

## [0.3.0] - 2026-11-19
- Random effects: `sender`, `receiver` (directed networks) and `sociality` (undirected and directed) with latentnet's priors (normal effects, scaled inverse chi-squared variance with scale 1 and 3 degrees of freedom; `sendervar()`, `senderdf()`, ... change them), random-walk Metropolis-Hastings updates of every effect with tuned step sizes and Gibbs updates of the variances, all in the native plugin. Results: `e(sender)`, `e(receiver)`, `e(sociality)`, `e(vsummary)`.
- Minimum Kullback-Leibler (MKL) positions are now the reported positions `e(Z)` (with `e(mkl_b)`, `e(mkl_effects)`), found in the plugin by a limited-memory BFGS maximisation of the likelihood of the posterior mean tie probabilities from the `mklstarts()` (default 5) draws closest to them. Deviations from latentnet: several starts, and a very weak ridge (1/1000 of the posterior precision) on positions and effects against runaway along flat directions. The mean of the draws aligned to the MKL positions is `e(Zmean)`; the posterior mean tie probabilities are `e(P)`.
- Bilinear latent term (`bilinear`, latentnet's `bilinear`).
- Post-estimation: `estat positions`, `estat mcmcdiag` (effective sample sizes and Geweke z-scores), `estat predict` (posterior mean tie probabilities as a valued network), and the plot command `nwlsmplot`.
- `savedraws()` now also saves the variance draws of the effects, and works with the intercept (it failed on the name `_cons` in 0.2.0).
- Tests run so far (Stata 17 BE on macOS only): `test_nwlsm_check` (likelihood, one-actor and one-effect updates, MKL gradient; 90 random states), `test_nwlsm_latentnet` (Sampson, random n = 40 with d = 2 and 3), `test_nwlsm_effects` (five models with sender, receiver, sociality, node covariate and bilinear terms against two latentnet chains each), `test_nwlsm_post`, `test_nwlsm_recovery`, `test_nwlsm_recovery_effects`, `test_nwlsm_errors`. Not run: Stata 18/19, Windows, Linux.
- Not implemented: latent clusters, valued networks.
