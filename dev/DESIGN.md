# nwlsm design (internal; not shipped)

Owner: Thomas U. Grund (sole author). GPL-3. Part of the nwcommands family; depends only on the core. Native C plugin only (no Mata fallback).

## Status (2026-11-19, version 0.7.0: stage 3, the social influence model, done; BIC and cluster selection, gof cutoff, worker team)
Stage 1, the sender/receiver/sociality and bilinear parts and the latent clusters of stage 2 are implemented (0.4.0, with chains and threads); valued networks (Poisson, binomial, normal), `estat gof`, `estat ppcheck` and trace plots are implemented (0.5.0). Stage 3 (the latent space model for social influence) is implemented in 0.6.0 (see below).

## Scope in stages
1. **Base model (latentnet "euclidean")**: binary one-mode networks (undirected and directed), logit P(y_ij = 1) = beta0 + sum_k beta_k x_ijk - |z_i - z_j|, z_i in R^d. Posterior by MCMC (random-walk Metropolis-Hastings on beta and z; Gibbs for the latent-position variance). Output: posterior means and intervals of beta, posterior sample, positions after Procrustes alignment, and the minimum-Kullback-Leibler (MKL) positions, as latentnet. Edge covariates from nwcommands networks.
2. Extensions that latentnet offers, in order of value: sender/receiver random effects (`sender`, `receiver`, `rsociality`), bilinear distance (`bilinear`), latent clusters (`G=`), valued networks (Poisson, ordinal). Each only after the previous one reproduces latentnet.
3. **Latent space model for social influence** (Sweet and Adhikari 2020, Psychometrika 85(2): 251-274): a Gaussian outcome at time t regressed on its lag and on the distance-weighted mean of the lagged outcomes of the G nearest actors in the latent space, estimated jointly with the latent space model of the ties. No reference software known (the authors wrote their own sampler); validate by simulation and by the numbers in the paper. Read the paper in full before implementing (we have read the model section and the first simulation example only).
   **Implemented (0.6.0), paper read in full** (PDF 2020PmetLSMInfluence.pdf in the owner's Downloads). Model (paper eq. 4-6): A_ij ~ Bernoulli(logistic(a0 - |z_i - z_j|)); y_k = x_k'b + b_infl m_k + e_k with m_k the weighted mean of the lagged outcomes of the G nearest actors, weights 1/d normalised (paper) or exp(-d) normalised (our option); priors b ~ N(0, 100), s2_y ~ InvGamma, z_i ~ N(0, tau I), tau ~ InvGamma; a0 fixed at 2 in the paper. Paper's sampler: random-walk MH for b and every z_i, Gibbs for s2 and tau. Ours: the same joint posterior; the outcome coefficients and s2_y by Gibbs (conjugate given the weights), the positions by the existing MH (acceptance ratio plus the outcome log-likelihood change), all in the C plugin (`influence_*` functions, plugin codes 5 and 6 for tests). Cost of a position move O(n): the weight row of the moving actor is recomputed, and for every other actor k only the entry of the mover changes unless the mover enters or leaves k's G nearest (or is the boundary actor), in which case row k is recomputed (O(n)); the sums are rebuilt once per sweep against rounding drift. Neighbour set definition: {j: d_kj <= G-th smallest distance}, so ties in the distances are consistent between the shortcut and the recomputation.
   Deviations (all in the help file): intercept a0 estimated (latentnet prior on the positions) instead of fixed; ties may have covariates; error variance prior default InvGamma(0.01, 0.01) not (10, 20) (the paper's prior has mean 2.2 against true 0.09 in its example); Gibbs instead of MH for the outcome coefficients; one network, one wave (no hierarchical version, no susceptibility indicator).
   Validation (cscripts/): `test_nwlsm_infl_check` (outcome log likelihood, weighted means and incremental position-move change against a direct Mata computation with sorting, 120 random states, shortcuts and recomputed rows), `test_nwlsm_infl_rec_*` (simulation from the exact model, 30 replications per scenario, coverage of the 95% intervals), `test_nwlsm_infl_nam` (against the exposure model with a fixed row-normalised network weight matrix via nwspmatrix and spregress), `test_nwlsm_infl_post` (results, threads bit-identical, chains, estat, errors).
4. Possible later: a cross-sectional binary-outcome version for the contagion study (not a published method; only if the paper needs it).

## Reference for stage 1
R package latentnet 2.12.0 (installed in the owner's R library; GPL). Defaults of `ergmm` (checked on 2026-11-19 with data(sampson), d = 2, no clusters, `fit$prior`): beta ~ N(0, var = 9) with `adjust.beta.var = TRUE` (the prior variance of a covariate coefficient is divided by the mean square of that covariate); Z variance prior (unclustered model): z_i ~ N(0, sigma2 I_d), sigma2 scale-inverse-chi-squared with scale Z.var = 0.125 * n^(2/d) and degrees of freedom Z.var.df = sqrt(n) (checked for n = 18, 20, 40, 80 and d = 2, 3 in dev/ref/prior_formulas.csv); Z.mean.var = 2 * Z.var (clusters only); sampling defaults: burn-in 10,000, 4,000 draws (sample.size), interval 10, target acceptance 0.234 with pilot runs tuning Z.delta = 0.6.
Compare: posterior means of beta, the posterior mean of the log likelihood, the matrix of posterior mean distances (invariant to rotation, reflection and translation, so it can be compared directly; the positions cannot), and the MKL positions after Procrustes. Because both are MCMC, agreement is statistical: use several seeds and compare with Monte Carlo error.

## Validation plan
- Likelihood: log-likelihood for fixed (beta, Z) equals an independent calculation in Stata/Mata and in R.
- Single-block Metropolis updates: acceptance ratios equal the full recomputation of the log posterior difference (test with random states).
- Recovery: simulate networks from known (beta, Z), check coverage of beta and recovery of the distance matrix.
- Agreement with latentnet on: Sampson (data(sampson), directed), a random undirected network with a node covariate, a larger network (n = 100).
- Speed: report time against latentnet on n = 50, 100, 200.

## Timing (2026-11-19, macOS, Stata 17 BE vs R 4.x latentnet 2.12.0, default settings: 10,000 burn-in, 4,000 draws, interval 10; directed network, d = 2, sender and receiver effects, MKL included)
| n | latentnet | nwlsm |
|---|---|---|
| 50 | 7.1 s | 4.4 s |
| 100 | 31.4 s | 19.0 s |
| 200 | 117.8 s | 76.8 s |
The run is bound by exp/log1p per dyad (about 7.7 ns per evaluation, 1e10 evaluations at n = 200); -O3 -fno-math-errno did not help. The n updates of the sender effects (and then of the receiver effects) are conditionally independent and could run in threads.

## Open items of 0.7.0 (2026-11-19)
- BIC (`estat bic`, `nwlsmselect`): latentnet's `bic.ergmm`: Y part -2 lpY(beta_mle | Z_mkl, effects_mkl) + p log(eff.obs) with find.mle over the coefficients only (Newton in Mata, post-estimation, binary family); Z part: without clusters -2 sum dnorm(Z, 0, sqrt(mean(Z^2) d)) + log(n d); with G clusters -2 llk(mbc.VII.EM on the MKL positions) + (G - 1 + d G + G) log n, the EM of ours: best of 15 k-means starts (Lloyd), 200 EM iterations, tolerance sqrt(eps), restart on a failed fit, Gused < G if no fit works (latentnet's warning). Effects parts -2 sum dnorm(e, 0, sd) + log n. Reference results `dev/ref/bic*` by `dev/ref_latentnet_bic.R`.
- Comparison with latentnet: R's k-means (Hartigan-Wong) and ours (Lloyd) can reach different mixture local maxima; with sender and receiver effects the MKL objective on Sampson has two local optima (effect sd 0.27/0.68 and 0.65/0.75; with the same posterior mean tie probabilities the first has the higher objective, -138.13 against -138.26 in R's evaluation); both packages start from the closest draw and land in either one (R: BIC 398-402 in 9 of 12 seeds, 455-602 in 3; nwlsm one start: 398-399 in 5 of 8 seeds, 440-443 in 3; mklstarts(8): 397-400 in 8 of 8). The BIC code is exact on R's own positions and effects (399.2 reproduced).
- gof `cutoff()`: tie = value above the cutoff in the observed and the simulated networks; plugin arguments usenet and thr appended to code 20.
- Worker team: `Pool` (persistent threads, mutex and condition variables; pthreads, Windows CRITICAL_SECTION and CONDITION_VARIABLE), created per chain when inner threads > 1 and for one MKL start; items dealt out k mod T, block sums added in block order, so the result is independent of the team size. Inner threads per chain now up to n^2 / 1500 (before n^2 / 10000). Gain is limited because the position updates are sequential: n = 100, 4 threads 2.1 s against 2.5 s.
- Hierarchical influence model and susceptibility indicator not implemented (see TODO.md for the reasons).
